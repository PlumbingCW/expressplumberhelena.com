When a burst pipe cannot wait until morning

Every second of flow adds to the damage, so a pipe that is actively discharging is an emergency at any hour. Calls that need action tonight:

  • Water is spraying, pouring or running down a wall or through a ceiling.
  • The main valve will not close, or is stuck partway.
  • A pipe in a crawlspace, garage or attic has split, and you can hear it running.
  • Pressure has dropped to a trickle after a hard freeze, and a pipe may have cracked while frozen.
  • Water is pooling around the water heater or washing machine connections after a sudden failure.

We take night and weekend repair calls, and we offer same-day appointments when available. If you have the water off and the area is safe, a small leak with the valve closed can sometimes wait for a morning visit. Tell us what you are looking at and we will help you decide.

Do this in the first ten minutes

  1. Shut off the main water valve. If it will not turn, tell us when you call and keep clear of the water.
  2. Open the lowest faucet and flush the top-floor toilets to relieve pressure.
  3. Turn off power to affected rooms if you can reach the breaker without standing in water. If you cannot, leave it and call the utility or 911.
  4. Move valuables, rugs and electronics out of the wet area, and start soaking up standing water with towels.
  5. Take photos and video of the damage before you clean up.
  6. Turn off the water heater (the gas valve or the breaker) if the burst is on the hot side, so the tank does not heat while empty.

What actually bursts, and why

Pipes fail in a few common ways, and the cause shapes the fix. Freezing is the best known: water expands as it turns to ice, and the pressure splits the pipe, usually at a weak spot or beside the frozen section.

Corrosion eats thin walls from the inside until a pinhole opens. Joint failures happen where a fitting was badly made, aged out, or pushed by movement. And impact or vibration can crack a line, especially one that is poorly supported.

Pipe material matters as well. Copper can split from freezing or wear through at bends, PEX can crack at fittings or from sunlight exposure, CPVC can turn brittle with age, and galvanized steel corrodes from the inside and restricts flow before it bursts. We look at the pipe and the failure before recommending anything, because a split in one place can mean the rest of the run is on its way out.

How the repair is done

We start by confirming the water is off and the failure is found. Then we cut out the damaged section and replace it with new pipe and fittings that match or suit the existing system. Mixing materials needs the right transition fittings, because copper and steel can corrode where they touch, and the wrong coupling will leak again.

After the repair we pressurize the line, check every joint, and watch the meter to confirm the leak is gone. Repairs often involve opening a wall or ceiling, so we cut access as small as we can. Drywall, paint and flooring afterward are usually separate trades.

If the failure is on a drain, not a pressurized supply, the approach is different. Drain breaks leak only when water flows, so symptoms are intermittent. A camera run can locate the break, and our sewer line repair page covers buried drain failures.

Copper, PEX, CPVC and galvanized: what you can expect

Each material has its own repair profile, and homeowners often ask which one they have. Copper is rigid and metallic, usually with soldered joints, and it repairs cleanly when the surrounding pipe is sound.

PEX is flexible and often red, blue or white, and a damaged section can be replaced with crimp or expansion fittings. CPVC is cream or light yellow and stiff, and it can crack when handled after aging. Galvanized is gray steel with threaded joints and a rough interior.

If you are unsure, take a photo of the pipe near the water heater or main valve and describe it on the phone. We would rather identify the pipe before we arrive than guess when we are standing in a wet room.

Frozen pipes

Pipes in unheated garages, attics, crawlspaces and exterior walls are the ones exposed when a hard freeze reaches central Alabama. If a pipe is frozen but has not burst, open the faucet slightly and warm the area with a hair dryer or space heater placed safely away from anything flammable. Never use an open flame.

If you find a frozen section, shut off the main valve before it thaws, since the split often shows up only when the ice melts and water begins to flow. Call us as soon as you see a wet spot or a drop in pressure.

Burst pipe or just a bad shutoff valve?

Not every flood is a burst pipe. A cracked supply stop under a sink or a failed washing machine hose can look just as dramatic. These are quick fixes, and the shutoff valve under the fixture often stops the flow without touching the main. If you can close the stop and the water stops, you may be dealing with a fixture repair, and our faucet repair page explains what that involves.

Toilets and water heaters have their own supply connections and can fail the same way. The water heater repair page covers tank leaks, and it is a good place to start if the water is coming from the tank itself.

What affects the cost of burst pipe repair

  • Where the pipe isAn exposed pipe in a garage is quicker to reach than one inside a finished wall or under a slab.
  • How much pipe is damagedOne split section is a short repair, while a run with several failures may call for a longer replacement.
  • Pipe materialThe materials and fittings required differ among copper, PEX, CPVC and galvanized pipe, and so does the labor.
  • Access and cleanupOpening walls or ceilings, drying out the area and patching surfaces add time and sometimes other trades.
  • Urgency and timingUrgency and scheduling also play a part, and we tell you the price before we start.

Sudden pipe bursts are often covered by homeowners' insurance for the resulting water damage, though gradual leaks may not be. Document everything with photos, keep receipts, and ask your insurer what paperwork they want from us. You will see a clear price before work starts.

What to have ready when you call

When the pipe was only the first symptom

A burst can reveal a larger problem. High water pressure, a failing pressure regulator, old pipe and repeated freezes all produce repeat failures. If a second pipe splits within a short period, ask us to check the pressure and look at the surrounding run, since repairing one spot while the rest is brittle often leads to another call.

We can also test pressure at an outdoor spigot, which takes only a minute. A reading well above what your fixtures are rated for points to a regulator problem, and that is a much easier fix than replacing pipe.
