What the water board's 2023 report means for your fixtures

Alabaster Water Board (205-663-6155) publishes a yearly water-quality report. The 2023 report lists hardness readings from 86 to 195 ppm depending on the source: 195 ppm for the board's own supply, 86 ppm for the Shelby County treatment plant and 95 ppm for the Talladega-Shelby plant. Which source reaches your tap depends on where you live, so ask the board.

Why does hardness matter at 2 a.m.? Mineral scale builds up inside water heater tanks, on heating elements and inside aerators. A tank that has scaled over works harder and can fail with little warning, which is a common reason for an overnight water heater repair call. If your hot water runs lukewarm or the tank makes popping sounds, schedule a look before it becomes a flooded closet.

A softener or a regular tank flush may help, but ask a plumber about your specific water before you buy equipment. If a tank is already leaking from the seams, repair will not save it and a planned water heater replacement is the safer route.

Shut off the main

Turn the main valve to closed. If the valve is stuck or you cannot find it, tell the dispatcher and call the Alabaster Water Board at 205-663-6155 about the meter shutoff.

Power down the wet zone

Keep out of any room where water touches outlets or the panel. Turn off the breaker only if you can reach it standing on dry floor, and call 911 if you see sparks.

Move what you can

Lift boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor. Roll back wet carpet corners and use towels or a wet vac to hold the water back.

Problems that bring Alabaster homeowners to the phone
