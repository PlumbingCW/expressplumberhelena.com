If water is rising in a tub, flooding a floor, or backing up out of a drain, stop using every sink, toilet, shower and washer in the house right now. Then call (855) 575-2890. Our line is open 24/7, so a Saturday night backup gets the same attention as a Tuesday morning clog.

When a clogged drain cannot wait until morning

Most clogs are slow and annoying. Some are not. A drain that is still full after an hour, water showing up in a second fixture, or a floor drain that is rising are all signs of a blockage deeper in the line.

After-hours calls usually come down to one question: is water going somewhere it should not? If a backup is damaging flooring, walls or belongings, call right away. If one bathroom sink drains slowly and nothing else is affected, you can usually wait for a daytime appointment.

Main line clogs: when everything backs up at once

The main line carries all of the home's waste out to the street sewer or a private lateral. When it clogs, the lowest fixtures in the house show it first. In many homes that is a shower, a tub, or a basement floor drain.

A main line clog is not something a plunger or store-bought chemical will fix. A plumber opens the line at a cleanout when one is available, runs a cable or jet through the blockage, and checks that water flows freely afterward.

If the blockage keeps coming back, a camera look at the pipe can show whether roots, a belly in the pipe, or damage is the real cause. See our sewer line cleaning page for how we handle the lines past the house.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Grease cools and hardens inside the pipe, food scraps catch on the buildup, and the passage narrows a little more each week. By the time the sink stops, the pipe is often coated several feet back.

A plunger can move a soft clog near the trap. A cable clears a hole through the blockage, but grease can leave a coating behind that rebuilds. For a heavily coated line, hydro jetting scrubs the pipe walls rather than just poking through the middle.

If you have a disposal and the sink backs up into the other basin, a garbage disposal problem may be involved. Our garbage disposal repair page covers jams, humming and leaks.

Tub, shower and bathroom sink drains

Hair and soap are the usual culprits in bathrooms. They wind together just below the drain opening and form a mat that traps more of both. A slow tub or shower drain is often a surface problem you can clear with a drain strainer and a plastic hair tool.

When a tub drains slowly and the problem returns within a few weeks, the clog is usually past the trap. A plumber clears it with a cable sized for the drain and checks that the overflow and tub drain are not leaking. Bathroom sinks clog in a similar way, with toothpaste, hair and soap sitting just below the stopper.

Clogged toilets that will not clear

One stubborn clog can often be handled with a flange plunger and patience. Add warm water to the bowl, seat the plunger fully, and push gently before pulling. If water rises toward the rim, do not flush again. Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet to stop the tank from refilling.

If the toilet clogs repeatedly, gurgles when other fixtures run, or backs up other drains when flushed, the problem may be farther down the line than the toilet itself. A toilet that clogs after a child dropped something in it may need to be pulled and checked. Our toilet repair page covers cracked bowls, failing flush parts and leaks.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Stop using water in the house, including washers and dishwashers
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a standing backup (it can splash and burn skin, and the plumber has to work around it)
  • Keep children and pets out of any water that came up from a drain
  • Move rugs, cardboard and belongings out of reach of the water
  • Find your cleanout cap if you know where it is, but do not open it under pressure
  • Turn off the washing machine and note which fixtures are affected
  • Put on rubber gloves and boots before cleaning up any contaminated water

Cable cleaning, jetting and camera work: which one fits

Not every clog needs the same tool. A plumber matches the method to the blockage, the pipe size, and the likelihood that the problem will return.

If a clog comes back after being cleared, ask for a camera look. Our sewer inspection page explains what the camera shows and why it matters for repeat backups. One cleaning that fixes a problem is routine. Repeated cleanings of the same drain are a sign something in the pipe needs attention.

Outdoor drains, storm drains and grease traps

Yard drains and French drains collect leaves, dirt and roots, and they clog when debris builds up at the grate or in the pipe. Clearing them is a cable or jetting job, and it can also be a landscaping fix if the drain is poorly graded. These lines are separate from your sewer system, so a clogged yard drain should not cause sewage to back up inside.

Commercial kitchens use grease traps to catch fats before they enter the sewer. A trap that is overdue for pumping can overflow or back up into floor drains. If you run a restaurant or food business in Helena, our commercial plumbing page covers after-hours service for business properties. Ask about grease trap service when you call, since the right approach depends on the trap's size and location.

Preventing the next clog

Prevention is mostly about what goes down the drain. Grease should go in the trash. Wipes labeled flushable still clog lines. Hair catchers in tubs cost little and save a lot of trouble. Run hot water after washing dishes to move soft residue along.

If you have a house with a long main line and a history of slow drains, schedule cleaning before the problem becomes a backup at an inconvenient hour. Scheduled work is easier on your household and your floors than an emergency in the middle of the night.

What affects the cost of drain cleaning

  • Location of the clogA clog near a fixture is faster to clear than one deep in the main line
  • AccessA cleanout makes the job simpler, while a line without one may need a fixture pulled
  • MethodCable cleaning, hydro jetting and camera work are priced differently
  • Severity and recurrenceHeavy buildup or roots take more time than a simple blockage
  • After-hours timingNight, weekend and holiday calls can carry different rates

You get a clear price before work starts, so you can decide before we begin. Water damage from a sewage backup may be covered by some homeowner policies, so ask your insurance provider what applies to your situation.
