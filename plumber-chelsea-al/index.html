A fast-growing town, and a plumbing line that never closes

Chelsea grew from 2,949 residents in 2000 to 14,982 in the 2020 census. That is rapid growth since 2000, and it means a lot of households are relatively new to their plumbing. When something fails, the likely culprits are supply lines, fixtures and water heaters, so those are the first places to check.

Who serves your address: water, sewer and permits

Water for much of Chelsea comes from Shelby County Water Services. Sewer is less simple. Sources we reviewed describe a private provider, Shelby Ridge Utility Systems, acquired by SouthWest Water in 2018, but those sources date from 2019, so check which utility serves your address before you call anyone about a main line problem.

This matters at night. If a main line backs up and the blockage is in the utility's pipe, the fix belongs to the utility. If it is in your lateral, it is yours. A plumber with a camera can show which side of the line has the problem, and the utility can confirm who owns what.

For permits, Chelsea Building Services works with Shelby County Development Services (205-620-6650) under a contract running through January 2027. Check with them about your job before work starts. We tell you when a repair or a replacement is likely to need a permit.

Emergency steps while the plumber is on the way

Before We Arrive

Shut the main water valve

Close the valve where the line enters the house. If water is still running after you close it, shut each fixture valve and tell us what you see.

Keep electricity away from water

Do not step into standing water near outlets or the panel. Turn off the breaker only from dry ground, and call 911 for sparks or a burning smell.

Stop using drains

If a sewer backup is rising, stop flushing, running the washer and using showers. Keep children and pets away from standing sewage.

Common emergency calls from Chelsea homes

A quick-growing town has a mix of problems, and the same few show up after dark again and again.

  • A main line that backs up into the lowest drain may need a camera and sewer line repair once the cause is clear.
  • Slow drains that suddenly stop usually need drain cleaning or, for stubborn buildup, hydro jetting.
  • A pipe that has split behind a wall is a burst pipe repair call, and a hidden slow leak calls for leak detection first.
  • A water heater that has stopped heating or is leaking at the tank belongs with our water heater repair team.

Builder-grade fixtures and early-life water heaters are good reasons to learn where each shutoff valve is. Walk through the house once, tag the main, the water heater supply and the toilet stops, and tell everyone in the household where they are. A few minutes now spares you the scramble at midnight, when a leak is already spreading and nobody can find the valve.

Choosing a licensed plumber in a hurry

An emergency is when homeowners hand over control. Before you do, look up the plumber on the Alabama Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board's public licensee lookup, and ask for a clear price before work starts. We are licensed and insured and we explain what is being repaired and why.

Ask about the repair itself. A patch on a pressurized line, or a clamp placed over a split, may hold until morning but may not be the permanent fix. A good plumber tells you what the temporary measure is and what comes next.
