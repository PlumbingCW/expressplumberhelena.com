A sewer camera inspection means a plumber feeds a small waterproof camera into your sewer line and watches the pipe on a monitor. You see roots, cracks, sags, and buildup as they actually are. It turns a guess about your sewer line into a clear picture.

Inspections are normally scheduled in advance. If sewage is backing up in your house now, that is a different situation. Stop using water and call our 24/7 line at (855) 575-2890, and we will clear the line first.

What a camera inspection shows

The camera travels along the pipe from a cleanout or another access point. As it moves, the plumber looks for signs of trouble and notes where they are along the line.

  • Roots growing through joints or cracks
  • Cracks, breaks, offsets, and separated joints
  • Bellies or sags where waste collects
  • Grease, scale, and buildup on the pipe walls
  • Collapsed or crushed sections
  • Pipe material and general condition
  • Connections where branch lines join the main

A locator on the surface can mark where the camera head sits, so you know the depth and position of any problem on your property.

Buying a home in Helena

A home inspector checks what is visible. Most do not run a camera through the sewer line, which runs underground out of sight. A line problem found after closing can turn into a significant repair, so many buyers add a camera inspection during the due diligence period.

Helena has grown quickly, with population rising from 3,918 in 1990 to 20,914 in 2020, so many homes are newer. Newer does not mean problem-free, since construction disturbance, settling and tree roots affect lines of any age. Homes in Old Town may have older pipe, and a camera look can show whether it is still sound.

If you are buying, ask your agent whether a sewer scope fits your timeline and contract. Our sewer line repair page explains what comes next if the camera finds damage.

How an inspection goes

What to expect on inspection day

  1. The plumber finds the cleanout or best access point
  2. If the line is dirty, it is cleaned enough for the camera to see
  3. The camera is pushed through the line while the plumber watches the monitor
  4. Problem spots are marked along the line
  5. You see what the camera found and what each finding means
  6. The plumber explains options, from doing nothing to repair

You should leave understanding what is in your line, not just holding a verdict. Ask to see the footage if you want to review it, and ask what each finding means for your home.

Inspection for repeat clogs and odors

If a drain keeps backing up after cleaning, something is likely wrong in the pipe. Roots, a sag, or a damaged joint will keep catching waste no matter how often the line is cleared. The camera finds it.

For heavy grease or root buildup, hydro jetting may be the next step. If the camera finds broken pipe, repair makes more sense. A sewage smell with no visible backup can also point to a cracked line or a dry trap, and the camera helps sort out which.

What happens after the inspection

If the camera shows a clean, sound line, no action is needed. If it shows buildup, cleaning may be the answer, and our drain cleaning page explains options. If it shows damage, repair choices range from spot fixes to trenchless work to replacement.

Findings do not have to be acted on the same day. Ask which problems are urgent and which can be monitored. A small root intrusion, for example, is handled differently from a collapsed section.
