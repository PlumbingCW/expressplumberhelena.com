How to Stop Clogged Drains for Good in Helena, AL

Most clogs are built slowly from grease, hair and things that should never have gone down the drain. These habits stop them before they start.

By the Helena Express Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026. Updated October 15, 2026.

You stop clogged drains by controlling what goes in. Keep grease and food scraps out of the kitchen sink, catch hair in the shower, flush only toilet paper and human waste, and run hot water after use. A few steady habits beat any chemical cleaner, and they cost almost nothing.

Clogs rarely appear overnight. They build in layers, a little hair, a little soap, a little grease, until the pipe narrows enough to block. That is why the same drain can clog again and again even after you clear it. The fix is to stop the layers from forming.

Start with the kitchen sink

The kitchen drain carries the worst mix: grease, starch, coffee grounds and food bits. Grease is the main offender. It goes down as a warm liquid, cools in the pipe, and coats the wall with a waxy layer that catches everything else.

Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing. Pour cooking fat into a can or jar, let it harden, and put it in the trash. Hot water and dish soap do not stop this. They only push the grease farther down the line, where it hardens out of reach.

Catch hair before it reaches the pipe

Hair is the top cause of shower and tub clogs. It wraps around the stopper or the first bend, catches soap, and forms a mat. A drain strainer costs little and catches most of it before it enters the pipe.

Empty the strainer after each use or at least every few days. Once a month, lift the stopper or strainer and pull out whatever has gathered underneath by hand with a disposable glove. This five-minute habit stops most tub clogs from forming, and it spares you from a slow drain that will not clear.

Toilets: only paper and waste

Wipes labeled "flushable" are a leading cause of main-line clogs. They do not break down like toilet paper, and they snag in bends and rough spots, where they gather with other debris. A single wipe may pass through, but repeated use builds a plug.

Keep a small trash can with a lid next to the toilet and put wipes, floss, swabs and hygiene products in it. Use a modest amount of toilet paper per flush. If your toilet flushes weakly or needs a second flush often, that is a sign of a partial blockage developing, and toilet repair may be the right next step.

A simple monthly routine

You do not need a calendar full of chores. A few small steps cover most of what causes trouble.

Monthly drain care that works

  • Empty and rinse every shower and tub strainer
  • Run very hot tap water in the kitchen sink for 30 seconds after the dishes
  • Scrub the disposal with ice and a lemon rind for smell, then run cold water
  • Check the lowest drain in the house for slow flow
  • Pull the bathroom sink stopper and clean out the gunk
  • Look under each sink for damp spots or a damp cabinet floor
  • Flush the toilet and watch for a weak flush

Skip the chemical drain cleaners

Chemical cleaners seem like a cheap fix, but they can harm more than they help. They may not clear a dense clog, and when they sit in a pipe they can damage some pipe materials, hurt seals and create a hazard for the next person to open the drain.

Never mix cleaners, and never plunge or snake a drain that has had chemical cleaner in it without telling the plumber first. Splashback is a real risk. A plain plunger or a hand drain tool is a safer first step for a minor clog. If a clog survives that, a professional drain cleaning is the next move.

When a clog is a sign of something bigger

One slow drain is a local problem. Several slow drains at once point to the main line. A gurgling toilet when you run the washer, or water backing up in the lowest tub or floor drain, are signs that the main sewer line is partly blocked.

In that case, stop using water in the house to avoid a sewage backup and call a plumber. Our drain cleaning team can clear the line and scope it if the clog keeps returning. A recurring clog is also a reason to have a sewer camera inspection, since roots or a damaged pipe can cause the same symptom.

What to do when a drain has already stopped

Not every clog is a failure of habit. Sometimes a child drops a toy, or a line has aged to the point that buildup holds fast. When that happens, use this order.

Clearing a stubborn drain safely

  1. Stop running water into the drain.
  2. Remove standing water with a cup so you can see the opening.
  3. Use a cup plunger with enough water to cover the cup, and work it with firm strokes.
  4. Try a hand drain tool or a drain snake on shower and sink clogs.
  5. If you have used a chemical cleaner, stop and tell the plumber.
  6. If more than one drain is slow or sewage appears, stop using water and call a plumber.

Helena homes and drain habits

Helena's population grew from 3,918 in 1990 to 20,914 in 2020, and many homes outside Old Town have only a few decades of use on their drain lines. That is long enough for grease and hair to build up where the line bends or meets another pipe, but not so long that the pipe itself is likely worn out. For most Helena homes, habits matter more than pipe age.

Homes in Old Town may have older drain lines where buildup holds more easily. In either case, prevention is cheaper than clearing a full blockage.
