Garbage Disposal Cost and Installation in Helena

A disposal swap is one of the quicker kitchen plumbing jobs, but the sink, the wiring, and the drain all change the price.

By the Helena Express Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026. Updated October 15, 2026.

Installing a garbage disposal costs more or less depending on four things: the horsepower of the unit, whether the sink already has a disposal, whether a power outlet is under the sink, and what the drain looks like once the old parts come out. A straight swap on a sink that already has a disposal is the simplest case. A first-time install in a sink that never had one takes more work.

This guide explains each factor so you can read a quote and know what you are paying for. We do not list dollar amounts, because the real number depends on your kitchen.

Start with the question: replace, or add new?

If you already have a disposal, replacement usually reuses the mounting hole in the sink, the electrical connection, and the drain. A new unit often drops into the same mounting ring or a compatible one.

If you have never had a disposal, the job adds an outlet or hardwired connection, a switch, a dishwasher connection point, and sometimes changes to the trap. That is why new installs take longer and cost more than swaps.

What affects the price

Garbage disposal cost factors

  • Motor sizeA larger motor handles tougher scraps and runs more quietly, but the unit costs more. Small motors are fine for light use.
  • Feed typeContinuous feed units run while a switch is on. Batch feed units run only with a stopper in place, which some families prefer for safety.
  • Electrical setupA sink with an outlet under it is quick. Adding an outlet, switch, or wiring run is done by a qualified person and adds to the job.
  • Drain and trap conditionCorroded or worn drain parts are replaced while the sink is open, which adds parts and time.
  • Dishwasher connectionIf the dishwasher drains through the disposal, the connection has to be made correctly or the dishwasher will back up.

Homeowners insurance usually does not pay for replacing a worn disposal. It may respond if a failed connection caused sudden water damage, so ask your carrier.

What the installation involves, step by step

How a disposal replacement usually goes

  1. Turn off power to the disposal at the breaker and confirm the switch does nothing.
  2. Disconnect the drain and dishwasher hose from the old unit.
  3. Release the mounting ring and lower the old disposal out.
  4. Clean the sink flange and set the new mounting assembly with fresh sealant.
  5. Hang the new disposal and lock it onto the mount.
  6. Reconnect the drain, dishwasher line, and electrical supply.
  7. Restore power, run water, and check every joint for drips.

Step one matters because the disposal is wired to household power. Never reach into a disposal with your hand, even when you think it is off.

When a repair makes more sense than a new unit

Not every humming or leaking disposal needs replacing. A jam is often cleared with a hex key turned at the bottom of the unit, and a tripped reset button is a one-minute fix. Leaks at the sink flange or the drain connection are often a gasket or a loose joint, not a dead motor.

If yours is jammed or tripping, our garbage disposal repair service can tell you whether fixing it is worth it. A unit with a seized motor or a cracked housing is better replaced.

Common mistakes that shorten a disposal's life

  • Grinding fibrous scraps such as celery or corn husks
  • Pouring grease or oil down the drain, where it cools and coats the pipe
  • Running hot water only briefly, so scraps sit in the line
  • Forcing large quantities in at once instead of feeding gradually
  • Using drain chemicals, which can damage the unit and the pipes

A disposal does not replace a trash can. The drain it feeds is the same one that carries everything else from your kitchen, and a clog downstream can hit the whole line. If slow drains keep returning, a drain cleaning visit looks at the pipe, not just the disposal.

Checklist before the installer arrives

Prepare the sink area

  • Empty the cabinet under the sink so there is room to work.
  • Note whether there is an outlet under the sink and where the switch is.
  • Take a photo of the dishwasher connection if you have one.
  • Run the disposal and write down what it does, such as humming, leaking, or silent.
  • Have the model number from the old unit if you can read it.
  • Find the breaker that feeds the kitchen, in case it needs to be shut off.

Safety checklist before touching a disposal

  • Switch off the disposal and unplug it or turn off its breaker
  • Never put a hand into the grinding chamber, even when it is off
  • Use tongs or pliers to remove a stuck item
  • If it hums but does not spin, stop and call rather than forcing it

What to expect from the quote

A quote should name the unit, say whether electrical work is included, and say who removes the old one. It should also tell you whether the dishwasher connection and the drain fittings are part of the price. If a quote is a single number with no detail, ask what is in it.
