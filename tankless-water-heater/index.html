A tankless water heater warms water only when you open a tap, so there is no tank of stored hot water to run out. It costs more to install than a standard tank, but it takes less space and can lower energy use. Call (855) 575-2890 if you want to install one, replace an older one, or fix one that is throwing a code or running cold.

Tankless units are more particular than tanks. They need the right gas line size, a correct vent, and clean water inside the heat exchanger. That is why service and installation both need a plumber who works with these units regularly.

How a tankless heater works

When you open a hot tap, flow sensors detect the water moving and fire a gas burner or energize electric elements. Water passes through a heat exchanger and leaves at the set temperature. When you close the tap, the unit shuts off.

This is why gas tankless units need a bigger gas supply than a storage tank. The burner has to heat the full flow all at once. Electric tankless units draw a lot of power for the same reason, so they need heavy-duty circuits and a panel that can handle the load.

Is tankless worth it for your home?

The honest answer depends on how you use hot water. According to the US Department of Energy, tankless water heaters last about 20 years and are 8% to 34% more efficient than storage tanks, depending on how much hot water you use. Homes that use a modest amount of hot water see the biggest percentage gain, while heavy users see less.

The biggest drawback is that a tankless unit can only heat so much water at once. If three showers and a dishwasher run together, a small unit may fall behind. Sizing it to your household matters, and we cover that during the quote.

Tankless water heater installation

Installing a tankless unit takes more than hanging it on a wall. The gas line may need to be larger, the vent has to be the right material and size, and a condensate drain is needed for high-efficiency models. Electric units may need a new breaker and heavy wiring.

When you switch from a tank, the old connections rarely line up with the new unit. We plan the gas supply, the vent path, the water lines, and the placement before work begins. For a standard tank swap without these changes, see water heater installation.

Check with the Helena building department about permits and inspection requirements for your address, since a gas and venting job may come with local rules. We will tell you what we see on our end.

Tankless water heater repair

Tankless problems are often about water quality, flow, or a safety sensor. Common complaints include a unit that delivers lukewarm water, a code that shows on the display, a unit that shuts off mid-shower, and a burner that will not light. Many of these are fixable without replacing the unit.

Error codes vary by brand, so the first step is writing down the code and the model number. A code can point to a blocked vent, an ignition issue, a water flow problem, or a scale-clogged heat exchanger. We read the code, test the unit, and explain the fix before we do it.

Leaks are the exception. A leak at a fitting or isolation valve can often be repaired, but a leak from inside the heat exchanger usually means that part has failed. If the unit has been in service a long time, we will go over repair against replacement honestly.

Tankless flush and descale service

A flush is the single most useful maintenance task for a tankless heater, and it is the one most homeowners miss. Minerals in the water gradually coat the inside of the heat exchanger. That coating reduces flow, causes error codes, and makes the unit work harder.

A flush circulates a descaling solution, usually white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved cleaner, through the heat exchanger using a small pump and the isolation valves. Then we flush the system with clean water, check the inlet screen, and test the unit. Many manufacturers recommend doing it on a regular schedule, and the right interval depends on your water.

If your tankless was installed without isolation valves, we can add them so future flushes are quick and easy. Some callers tell us a previous plumber declined to service their unit. We service tankless heaters and can tell you whether yours needs a flush, a part, or a replacement.

Signs your tankless unit needs attention

Look for these signs, and call us if any of them show up:

  • Hot water that runs cool or inconsistent, especially at low flow
  • Error codes on the display or blinking lights
  • A louder than normal sound, like rumbling or kettling from inside the unit
  • Longer waits for hot water at the tap
  • A burner that clicks but does not light, or soot near the vent
  • Visible scale or white buildup at the water inlet screen

A unit that shows these signs for months can lose efficiency and may need a more expensive repair later. Flushing early is usually cheaper than waiting for the heat exchanger to fail.
