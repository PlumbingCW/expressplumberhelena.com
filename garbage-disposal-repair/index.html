Why a disposal fails

A disposal is a small motor spinning a grinding plate under your sink. It fails in a few predictable ways, and each one has a different fix. Knowing which you have saves a wasted trip.

Disposal symptoms and what they usually mean

Leaks, jams, and backups: when it cannot wait until morning

Call the same night if water is coming from the unit, if the sink is backing up into the other basin, or if wastewater is reaching the cabinet floor. A leak at the housing usually means the unit needs replacement. A backup means the clog is past the disposal, and the kitchen drain line is where we will work.

If your dishwasher drains through the disposal, a clog can send dirty water back into the dishwasher. Stop running it and call. After-hours dispatch handles these the same as daytime calls.

What not to do

Do not pour drain cleaner into a jammed or backed-up disposal. The chemical sits in the chamber and can burn skin when a plumber opens the unit. Do not run the disposal with water standing in the sink and the unit leaking.

  • Keep hands and tools out of the chamber.
  • Do not reset repeatedly if the unit trips over and over.
  • Skip grease, coffee grounds, and fibrous scraps like celery or corn husks.
  • Never mix chemical cleaners from different bottles.

Jammed unit or clogged line?

Callers often describe a disposal problem when the real issue is further down. If the disposal runs fine but the sink drains slowly, the clog is likely in the trap or branch line. If the disposal hums or the water leaks from the unit, the disposal itself is the issue. We check both before replacing anything.

Kitchen sinks are a leading source of drain calls because grease and food solids cool and stick inside the line. If that is your situation, our drain cleaning page covers what clearing the line involves, and hydro jetting is the option for stubborn buildup.

Repair, replace, or install a new disposal

A jam or a tripped motor can often be cleared. A leaking housing, a seized motor, or a unit that has failed more than once is usually a replacement. Tell us if you want a bigger motor for heavy use.

If your kitchen never had a disposal, we can install one. That means a switch or outlet, a drain connection, and a dishwasher knockout opened properly. Mention it when you call so the right parts are on the truck.

Right now, before we arrive

  1. Switch off the disposal at the wall.
  2. Stop running water into the sink.
  3. Empty the cabinet under the sink and set a bucket under any drip.
  4. Wipe up standing water so it does not reach the cabinet floor.
  5. Leave the reset button alone after one press.
  6. Call and tell us if the sink is leaking, backing up, or both.
