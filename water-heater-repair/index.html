Gas water heater repair

Gas heaters depend on a flame, a safe supply of gas, and a clear vent path, so repairs are about all three. A pilot that keeps going out is often a worn thermocouple or a dirty pilot assembly. The gas control valve can also fail, and that is a part replacement rather than a quick adjustment.

If your pilot went out once, you can follow the lighting instructions printed on the heater. If it will not stay lit, or if you ever smell gas, do not keep trying. Leave the house, call 911 or your gas utility, and call us once everyone is safe. A blocked vent or a back-drafting heater can also release carbon monoxide, so a working CO alarm near the heater is worth having.

Electric water heater repair

Electric tanks have two heating elements and two thermostats, and most no-heat calls come down to one of them. A tripped breaker is the easy case, and it is worth checking before you call. A repeat trip usually means a shorted element, which needs a replacement and not just a reset.

Lukewarm water that runs out quickly often points to the lower element, while no hot water at all often points to the upper element or thermostat. Water and electricity do not mix, so do not open the access panels yourself if there is any dampness around the tank. Shut off the breaker and let us handle the testing.

Hybrid and heat pump water heater repair

Hybrid heat pump heaters use a small compressor system plus backup electric elements, so they have more parts than a standard tank. Common issues include error codes, fan or airflow problems, a clogged condensate drain, and a unit that quietly switches to its backup elements and drives up the power bill. They also need open space around the unit to pull in air.

We work on hybrid units and will check the manufacturer's code list, the airflow, the condensate line, and the backup elements. Some failures inside the sealed heat pump system need the manufacturer or an authorized service agent, and we will tell you plainly if that is the case.

When a leak means the tank is done

Water heater leaks are the problem we hear about most. The cause is usually one of two things. If the water comes from a fitting, valve, or supply line, the leak can often be repaired. If it comes from the tank body, the inner shell has corroded through, and no patch will hold.

A leak that seems to come from under the tank may also be condensation or a nearby pipe, so a plumber checks before blaming the tank. If the unit is more than about 10 to 15 years old, which the US Department of Energy gives as typical for storage tanks, and the tank itself is the source, replacement is the sensible choice. See our water heater replacement page for how that works.

A heater failure that cannot wait until morning

Some heater failures are emergencies and should not wait for daylight. Call our night and weekend line if you have any of the following:

  • Water running out of the tank or filling the drain pan
  • A relief valve that is spraying or discharging steadily
  • Scalding water at the taps even after you lower the setting
  • Gas odor, soot around the heater, or a CO alarm sounding
  • A heater in an attic or on an upper floor that is dripping onto ceilings below

If you have no hot water and no leak, it may be able to wait for a morning visit, but you do not have to guess. Call and we will help you decide, and we can book the next available appointment.

What to check before you call

A few quick checks are safe and sometimes solve the problem. Take only the steps that feel comfortable.

Quick checks for no hot water

  • Check that the breaker for an electric heater has not tripped
  • Look at the gas control to confirm it is set to on (gas heaters)
  • Check whether the pilot light is lit, and follow the label if you relight it
  • Confirm the water supply valve above the heater is fully open
  • Note any error code or blinking light, and the brand and model on the label
  • Write down when the problem started and whether anything changed beforehand

If any step shows water, gas odor, or damaged wiring, stop there and call. Do not remove covers on a wet electric heater.

Repair or replace: the honest comparison

We would rather fix a young heater than sell you a new one. Most failed elements, thermostats, thermocouples, and relief valves are modest repairs on a heater that is otherwise in good shape. A tank that is old, rusty, or leaking is usually better replaced.

Age matters because the US Department of Energy puts storage tank life at about 10 to 15 years. A repair on a 13-year-old tank can buy time, but the next failure may be the tank itself. We will tell you where your unit sits in that range and let you decide. If you are planning a new unit without an emergency, see water heater installation.

Maintenance that prevents repeat calls

Tanks collect sediment from the water, and that sediment bakes onto the bottom, reduces efficiency, and makes the tank noisy. An annual flush helps many heaters last longer. A plumber can also check the relief valve, the shutoff valve, and the condition of the anode rod.

The anode rod is a metal rod inside the tank that corrodes first so the tank steel lasts longer. If it is spent, the tank corrodes faster. Replacing a worn rod early can add life to a tank that is otherwise healthy, though it will not save a tank that has already started leaking.

What affects the cost of water heater repair

  • The failed partA thermostat or element costs less than a gas control valve or a heat pump component.
  • Heater typeGas, electric, and hybrid units have different parts and different labor.
  • AccessA heater in an attic or tight closet takes longer to reach and work on.
  • After-hours timingNight, weekend, and holiday visits are priced differently from daytime appointments.
  • Related issuesA corroded valve, supply line, or drain pan may need replacing at the same time.

If a heater leak damaged your floors or walls, your homeowners policy might cover the water damage even if it does not cover the heater. Ask your insurer, and photograph the damage before cleanup.
