Emergency plumber

When you need a plumber right now

A real plumbing emergency is any problem that is damaging your home, cutting off water you cannot do without, or putting your health at risk. Water pouring from a broken pipe, sewage coming up through a floor drain, a water heater leaking across the garage and a toilet that will not stop overflowing all qualify. If that is your night, call (855) 575-2890 and stay on the line while a dispatcher sorts out what is happening.

Helena Express Plumbing runs a 24/7 emergency line for Helena, Alabama and the nearby towns of Pelham, Alabaster, Chelsea and Calera. The line is answered on weekends, holidays and in the middle of the night, because pipes do not wait for business hours. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and you hear a clear price before work starts.

First, protect the house

Find the main water shutoff and turn it clockwise until it stops. Open one faucet at the lowest level of the house to drain the pipes, and keep everyone away from standing water near outlets or appliances. If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911 and then the gas utility emergency line. Do not call us first for a gas smell.

Once the water is off, the next call is to us. Tell the dispatcher what you see, where the water is coming from and whether you have already found the shutoff. That short conversation decides what kind of plumber and which tools get sent, so it saves time on arrival.

Problems we handle after hours

Not every plumbing problem is a middle-of-the-night call, but many are. These are the ones that most often justify a late or weekend dispatch in a Helena home.

  • A burst or leaking pipe, including a supply line under a sink or a pipe in an attic or crawl space
  • A main drain or sewer line backup, with water or sewage rising in tubs, showers or floor drains
  • A water heater that is leaking from the tank or has failed completely in cold weather
  • A running or overflowing toilet that the shutoff valve on the wall will not stop
  • A suspected underground leak, such as a wet patch in the yard or a meter that keeps spinning with everything off

Helena

Plumbing in a fast-growing city

Helena has grown quickly. The population was 3,918 in 1990, reached 10,296 in 2000, climbed to 16,793 in 2010 and stood at 20,914 in the 2020 census. The Census Bureau's July 2025 estimate is 23,388.

For plumbing, that means most homes outside Old Town are newer than 1990, and a storage tank water heater from the early years of that growth is at or past the 10 to 15 years the US Department of Energy gives for its life.

The city sits in Jefferson and Shelby counties, with the Cahaba River and Buck Creek running through it. Water and sewer service comes from the City of Helena Utilities Board, also called the Helena Water Board, which you can reach at (205) 663-1670.

If a leak is on the utility's side of your meter, the board is the one to call, and if it is on your side, you are the one with a problem and we are the right call.

The Census Bureau puts the owner-occupied rate at 91.1 percent and the median owner-occupied home value at $330,700. Owners who stay put tend to deal with the same water heater, the same builder-grade faucets and the same sewer lateral for years. That is why a tank that is a decade old deserves a look before it fails on a holiday weekend.

Helena places and the plumbing they mean

Old Town Helena

The oldest part of the city, near Buck Creek and the Old Town Amphitheater. Older homes here are the ones most likely to have aging drain lines worth a camera look.

Dearing Downs

A large, established subdivision built in phases. Neighbors with water heaters of similar age may be facing the same replacement decision.

Riverwoods

A neighborhood with sidewalks and a pool. Leaks under landscaping and irrigation lines are worth separating from house plumbing.

Old Cahaba

One of the newer areas. Warranty-era fixtures can fail early, and a quick repair is often better than a rebuild.

Lake Davidson and Buck Creek

Creekside lots can stay wet after storms, so a damp yard needs a leak check before you assume it is rain.

Cahaba Lily Park area

Homes near the river can see standing water after heavy rain, so check whether a wet yard is runoff or a leak before you dig.

Pricing

What changes the price of an emergency plumbing visit

What affects the cost

  • The problem itselfA clogged fixture drain, a failed supply line and a collapsed sewer section are different jobs with different tools, parts and time.
  • AccessA pipe in an open crawl space is quicker to reach than one behind a finished wall or under a slab, and access changes labor.
  • Parts and equipmentWater heaters, valves and fixtures vary by size, fuel type and brand. Specialty tools such as a camera or jetter add to the job.
  • Time of dayAfter-hours and holiday dispatches carry a different rate than a scheduled daytime visit, and you are told that up front.
  • Scope of the fixA quick repair that stops the damage tonight may be followed by a larger planned repair, and you choose each step.

Many homeowner policies cover sudden water damage but not wear or maintenance, so ask your insurer what applies before you file. Keep photos of the damage and your invoice. We can describe the work in plain language to help your claim, and we do not tell you what your policy will pay.
